= Types and assurance in applied systems

Potential applications of type-theory work include preventing category errors
between cash and accruals, periods and currencies, observed and illustrative
values, restricted and available funds, and commitments and recognised
liabilities. These are design opportunities, not established proofs.

A model can carry provenance and evidence status with each value. Operations
could require compatible units and periods, preserve dependence information and
refuse to turn a missing value into a number. A decision result could state the
assumptions and evidence on which it depends.

Research questions:

* Which distinctions should be enforced by types, and which require runtime or
  organisational review?
* Can scenario composition preserve cash reconciliation and avoid double counting?
* How can revisions invalidate dependent conclusions without erasing history?
* What assurance connects a checked mathematical model to a deployed spreadsheet
  or program and its real-world inputs?

Formal proofs cannot establish the truth of unverified inputs or the adequacy
of a causal model. Keep proof obligations and receipts separate from empirical
validation. Follow the estate's canonical proof policy for any implementation;
for first-party Rust, explicitly allocate Creusot/Pearlite implementation
contracts and complementary Verus obligations, recording inapplicable or pending
work.

The cohort model (`verification/proofs/agda/Applied/Cohort.agda`) is the first
test of these ideas. Each value carries its evidence status, a missing value
stays missing, and every worked example is checked by Agda. Its general
properties (obligations APP-1 to APP-4 in `docs/status/PROOF-NEEDS.adoc`) are
proved in Agda and re-proved in Rocq and Lean. A check of a model on an example is not a proof of the model, and neither
establishes the truth of real inputs.
