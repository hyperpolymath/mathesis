= Architecture Decision Record: 0004-claims-registry

== 4. A theorem-level claims registry that derives grades and never assigns them

Date: 2026-11-09

=== Status

*Accepted (ratified by owner 2026-11-10).* The owner ruled through the
selector on 2026-11-10 that the change which amends this record to name the
canon reader (re-anchoring plan WO 2.2) also sets this status, and that the
owner's merge of that change is the ratification. Until then the status was
"Proposed, awaiting owner ratification", and the registry view said that the
slice was proposed.

=== Context

The atlas (`docs/atlas/README.adoc`) stops at repository-level counts and says
so: "The next slice requires a separately reviewed, source-anchored claims
registry." The architecture brief
(`docs/proposals/estate-mathematical-architecture.adoc`) names that slice as
Stage 1 (schema decision) and Stage 4 (proof-level indexing), with `registry/`
and `schemas/` directories; its proof grades were adopted, corrected, as
`PROOF-POLICY.adoc` §8 (owner ruling 2026-11-04), which binds what a registry
may say:

* §8.4: grades are *derived*, not asserted; a hand-written grade the receipts
  do not support is drift; a census, never a percentage.
* §8.5: each theorem's grade is recorded in its *home repository*; "Mathesis
  may catalogue grades. It derives them from those receipts and does not assign
  them."
* `docs/standards/AUTHORITY.adoc` (this repository): pin claims to source
  commits, checker versions, assumptions and receipts; never infer a missing
  field from a green badge; proof receipts stay in their home repositories.

So the registry's job is narrow: point at a claim in its home repository at an
exact commit, fetch that repository's own machine receipt, recompute the grade
by §8.2, and report disagreements. It is a checker of home-repository claims,
not a second ledger.

=== Decision

==== Record (the schema)

`docs/registry/claims.json` is one I-JSON/JCS array. Each record has exactly
these fields; `scripts/registry/claims-registry.mjs` `validate` enforces them
and the tests prove each failure.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Field |Meaning

|`id` |`<owner>/<repo>#<claim>`; unique.
|`repo` |The home repository. It must be `public` in
`docs/catalogue/census/repos.jsonl`: the registry never names a private
repository, so it cannot leak one (brief §7).
|`claim` |The claim's id in the home repository's proof-status file (`N-7`, `L-6`).
|`kind` |`theorem` (graded, §8.1), `demonstration` (a TESTED example) or `assumption`
(an ASSUMED hypothesis). Only theorems are graded.
|`statement` |The statement in the home repository's own wording. Mathesis
does not restate mathematics.
|`status_doc` |The home repository's proof-status file.
|`agda` |For a theorem: `{module, names}`, the Agda source of truth (§1). `null`
otherwise.
|`source_commit` |The full 40-character commit the record describes.
|`receipt` |`{path, sha256}` of the home repository's machine receipt at that
commit, or `null` when it has none.
|===

A record holds *no grade, token, leg or flag*. Those are read from the receipt.

==== Derivation (the canon reader, PROOF-POLICY §8.5.1)

Amended 2026-11-10 (re-anchoring plan WO 2.2, owner ruling D-5 (a)). The
registry has no grading rule of its own. The canon reader of PROOF-POLICY
§8.5.1 derives each grade: `readProofReceipt` in `metadatastician/standards`
`1-formats/deed/tools/deed_lint.js`. The registry pins the reader by its
standards commit and by the sha256 of that file (`READER` in
`scripts/registry/claims-registry.mjs`), and refuses any other file.

For each record, the tool fetches the receipt at `source_commit`, refuses it
if its sha256 differs from the pinned digest, and derives:

. not a theorem → `ungraded` (§8.1);
. no receipt → `ungraded`;
. the reader refuses the receipt → `ungraded`, with the reader's reason. This
  includes a receipt that records a `:pwg` its legs and receipts do not support
  (drift, §8.4), and a receipt that is not in the §8.5.1 shape;
. the receipt is for another repository, or the claim is absent from it →
  `ungraded`;
. otherwise the grade that the reader derives for the claim, from `PWG-3` to
  `PWG-1` (§8.2).

Until this amendment the registry carried its own copy of the §8.2 rules, read
only the Agda side, and never derived `PWG-1`.

==== View, gate and tooling

* `docs/registry/registry.adoc` is generated (`just registry`) and checked
  (`just registry-check`, and CI `proven-tests.yml`): a stale view, a digest
  mismatch, or a reader that is not the pinned file fails. A receipt that the
  reader refuses shows as `ungraded` with the reader's reason. The view opens
  with a §8.4.7 census line.
* The tool is Bun, beside the atlas (`scripts/atlas/census-atlas.mjs`), with
  hermetic tests (`tests/registry/claims-registry.test.mjs`, `--receipts-dir`
  fixtures in the §8.5.1 shape): the grades that the reader derives, each
  withheld reason, a refused receipt, the reader pin, digest refusal,
  validation failures, and "never a percentage". The tests and the tool need
  `MATHESIS_STANDARDS`, a standards checkout at the reader pin, and fail
  without it.
* Receipts are fetched from `raw.githubusercontent.com` at the pinned commit,
  never vendored (AUTHORITY: receipts stay home).

==== Pilot rows

Seventeen claims from the two repositories whose proofs this work audited:
`hyperpolymath/secret-types` (N-1 to N-9) and `metadatastician/lintel` (L-1 to
L-7, A-1), each pinned at its `main` of 2026-11-09. At those commits the
derivation gives *0 of 12 theorems graded*, and says why:

* secret-types `eee4e40`: the receipt records the flag set without
  `--cubical-compatible` (waiver W-1), and uses the pre-§8 "grade-3" wording.
  hyperpolymath/secret-types#32 puts the flag in force and writes the §8.5
  receipt; once it merges, bumping the pin should derive `PWG-3`.
* Lintel `a0f2bae`: no machine receipt exists in the repository.

That the pilot reports zero is the point: it is what the evidence supports today.

On 2026-11-10 (WO 2.2) the reader still gives 0 of the 12 pilot theorems a
grade. It refuses the secret-types receipt at `eee4e40` ("unexpected clause
(identity) in the receipt"): the receipt predates the §8.5.1 shape. The same
change adds `hyperpolymath/mathesis#THY-1`, pinned at `9aa1662`, the commit
that holds its CI receipts. The reader derives `PWG-1` for it.

=== Alternatives considered

* *Store grades in the registry.* Rejected: §8.4 and §8.5 forbid assigning
  them, and a stored grade drifts silently when the home repository moves.
* *Vendor receipts into Mathesis.* Rejected: AUTHORITY keeps receipts home;
  a pinned digest gives the same tamper-evidence without a copy.
* *A JSON Schema file validated by a library.* Deferred: it adds a pinned npm
  dependency for checks the 40-line `validate` already makes and tests. A
  schema file can be generated from `validate` later if a consumer needs one.
* *A ruling in `standards` first.* Possible, slower: the registry's authority
  would sit beside PROOF-POLICY §8.5. Chosen instead: ratify here, because the
  registry only *reads* the policy and adds no rule to it.

=== Consequences

* Mathesis can answer "which theorems, at which grade, why not higher" for the
  piloted repositories, with every answer traceable to a commit and a digest.
* A home repository whose receipt overstates its grade shows as `ungraded`,
  with the reader's reason, in the view (§8.4.2: "a gate or a bot may recompute
  it at any time and reports the computed value"). Until the WO 2.2 amendment
  it turned this repository's CI red instead.
* The census rows and the atlas are unchanged; the registry sits beside them.
* After ratification, in order:
  . Lintel: move its real ledger into `docs/status/PROOF-STATUS.adoc`, retire
    the template boilerplate, adopt the §2.1 invocation and add a receipt;
  . `scripts/census/build-rows.sh`: prefer `PROOF-STATUS` over `PROOF-NEEDS`
    when choosing a repository's status document, then refresh the two rows;
  . list both repositories as the first §2 reconciliation pilot in
    `docs/status/CROSS-PROVER-RECONCILIATION.adoc`;
  . bump the pilot pins as those changes land.
