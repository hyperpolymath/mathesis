= Architecture Decision Record: 0005-jcs-json-receipt
:status: PROPOSED

== 5. The claims registry also reads a proof receipt written as canonical JCS I-JSON

Date: 2026-11-16

=== Status

*Proposed. It is not accepted until the owner accepts it.* It follows the owner's ruling of
2026-11-16: trope-checker does not emit a praxis deed. Instead, the reader in
Mathesis also accepts a receipt that is canonical JCS I-JSON. This record says
how. The prototype (`scripts/registry/json-receipt.mjs`, tests in
`tests/registry/json-receipt.test.mjs`) is *not wired* into
`claims-registry.mjs`. The registry reads praxis deeds only until this record
is accepted, and no trope-checker row is added to `claims.jsonl` here.

=== Context

ADR 0004 pins each record's receipt as `{path, sha256}` and derives the grade
with the canon reader of PROOF-POLICY §8.5.1. That reader is `readProofReceipt` in
`metadatastician/standards` `1-formats/deed/tools/deed_lint.js`, pinned by commit
and digest in `READER`. The reader reads praxis deeds: S-expressions, with each
line at most 998 octets.

`hyperpolymath/trope-checker` publishes its evidence as `verification/pwg.json`.
That file is I-JSON (RFC 7493) in canonical JCS form (RFC 8785), and
`scripts/pwg-check.sh` gates it. The owner rules out a deed there. Given to the
pinned reader as it stands, the file fails at once: "line 1: line is 26347 octets".

Two constraints bind any answer:

* PROOF-POLICY §8.4 and §8.5, and ADR 0004: grades are derived, never asserted.
  Mathesis derives them from receipts and has no grading rule of its own
  (WO 2.4).
* AUTHORITY: receipts stay home. The registry fetches them at a pinned commit
  and does not copy them.

=== Decision (proposed)

==== The JSON receipt

A JSON receipt is the §8.5.1 proof receipt, written field for field in
I-JSON, with *no grade*. The field names are those of the record that the
canon reader returns (`snake_case`). Nothing else is allowed.

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|JSON field |§8.5.1 deed field |Note

|`profile` |`:profile proof-receipt` |Must be `"proof-receipt"`.
|`schema_version` |`:schema-version` |`"1.0.0"`.
|`repository` |`:repository` |`OWNER/REPO`.
|`source_commit` |`:source-commit` |40 lowercase hex.
|`trusted_base[]` `{prover, module, name, binding}` |`(trusted-base (primitive …))` |Required array. `[]` means the deed omits the clause.
|`echoes[]` `{source, image, witness, tree_sha256?, inputs?[] {prover, sha256}}` |`(echo …)`, `(inputs …)` |Required array, possibly empty. Commits are 40 hex.
|`claims[]` `{id, legs[], controls?[]}` |`(claim :id …)` |*No* `pwg` and *no* `foundation_diverse`.
|`legs[]` `{prover, route, token?, state?, reason?, receipts[]}` |`(leg …)` |`receipts` is required, and empty for a waived or unavailable leg.
|`receipts[]` `{run, commit, checker, invocation, inputs_sha256?, state, reason?, image?, inputs_manifest?[], imports?[], axioms?[], result}` |`(receipt …)` |`commit` is 40 hex. `image` is the digest-pinned image the checker ran in. A counted witness needs it for `PWG-1` (§8.2, owner ruling 2026-11-16).
|`imports[]` `{repository, commit, inputs_sha256, files[] {path, sha256}}` |`(import … (file …))` |A pinned repository that the run read (§8.5.1). Leave the field out when there is none: `[]` is refused, so there is one form.
|`controls[]` `{path, prover, expect, receipts[]}` |`(control … (receipt …))` |A negative control that the checker must refuse (§8.5.4). It is recorded, never graded. `[]` is refused, as for `imports`.
|`inputs_manifest[]` `{path, sha256}` |`(inputs-manifest (file …))` |
|`axioms[]` `{name, disposition}` |`(axioms (axiom …))` |An absent field and `[]` mean different things, as in the deed. `[]` means the checker reported no axiom.
|`result` |none |The only field with no deed counterpart: `pass` or `fail`. `current` requires `pass`, and `fail` requires `regressed`. In the deed, `:state` carries this. For a control receipt, `pass` means that the checker refused the file, as expected.
|===

`:canonical-name` and `:beholding-chora` belong to the deed envelope. The
reader's record carries neither, so the JSON form leaves them out.

==== What the reader refuses

The JSON layer refuses a file:

. that is not strict UTF-8, or that starts with a byte order mark;
. whose bytes differ from the JCS form of its value. One final LF is allowed,
  as `scripts/check-ijson-jcs.sh` allows. This rule also refuses duplicate
  keys, unsorted keys, insignificant whitespace and escapes that JCS does not
  use. *Canonicality is a hard requirement*, because the `sha256` pin of
  ADR 0004 needs exactly one byte form for each value;
. that holds a lone surrogate or a noncharacter in a key or a value (RFC 7493 §2.1);
. with an *asserted grade*: a key `pwg`, `grade`, `grades`,
  `foundation_diverse` (or `foundation-diverse`, `foundationDiverse`) at any
  depth. The reason given says that the registry derives the grade;
. with an unknown field at any depth, or a missing required field, or `null`
  in place of a value (an optional field is left out instead);
. with a number or a boolean anywhere. No field of the form has either type;
. with a commit that is not 40 lowercase hex: short, abbreviated or uppercase;
. with a string value that is empty, holds a control character (a trailing
  newline, a tab or a CR), or is longer than 900 UTF-8 octets;
. with a symbol-valued field (`prover`, `route`, `token`, `state`,
  `disposition`, `witness`) that is not letters, digits and hyphens. This
  stops deed syntax from being smuggled in;
. with a `result` outside `pass | fail`, a `current` receipt that failed, or a
  `fail` that is not `regressed`.

After the JSON layer, the *pinned canon reader* refuses whatever it refuses in
a deed: values outside its closed vocabularies (no coercion, so `PROVEN` is
refused where `proven` is meant), an Agda leg whose route is not `source`, a
manifest whose digest is not its `inputs_sha256`, duplicate claim ids, bad
echoes, and the rest.

==== How the grade is derived

The prototype adds no grading rule. It writes the receipt as §8.5.1 deed text
and hands that text to the pinned reader, which runs after `loadReader()` has
checked its digest. The reader checks a recorded `:pwg` and
`:foundation-diverse` against the values it derives, and refuses a mismatch.
The prototype uses that check as an oracle. For each claim it tries the closed
grade vocabulary, five grades times two flags. Exactly one pair is accepted,
and that pair is the derived grade. If the reader accepts no pair, the
prototype reports the reader's refusal. If it accepts more than one, the
prototype fails loudly. It then reads the whole receipt once more with the
derived pairs, so that the checks across claims also run. The result is the
canon reader's own record. The deed text is never stored.

Tested equivalence: four receipts round-trip from deed to record to JSON to
record and come back byte-identical under JCS:

* this repository's `theory_praxis.deed`;
* the standards worked examples `example_praxis.deed` and `echo_praxis.deed`
  (`PWG-1` through an echo, with each current receipt naming its image);
* the echo example with an `(import …)` and a `(control …)` added, its digests
  computed.

The tests compare each grade with the one the canon reader derives from the
deed. They do not hard-code grades, because grades move with the pin. Under
reader `940a27af`, this repository's receipt derives `PWG-2ii` for all five
claims: it names no checker image, and `PWG-1` now needs one.

Amended 2026-11-23. The merge of `main` moved the reader pin from `e5a39d7`
to `940a27af`, and that reader added `:image`, `(import …)` and `(control …)`.
On the merged tree the round-trip test failed. The JSON form dropped `:image`,
so the echo example derived `PWG-2ii` instead of `PWG-1`. The form now carries
all three fields. This is what the equivalence test is for: when the canon
reader grows a field, the JSON form fails loudly until it follows.

==== Wiring, once accepted

`readReceipt` in `claims-registry.mjs` would dispatch on the pinned path. A
`*_praxis.deed` would go to the reader directly, as now. A `*.json` would go
through `readJsonReceipt` and then the same reader. The rest of the derivation
would not change: the digest pin, `derive()`, and the §8.5.3 git checks. The
git checker in standards reads deeds only. A JSON receipt that reaches `PWG-1`
would therefore be catalogued as `PWG-2ii` ("§8.5.3 git checks cannot run on
a JSON receipt") until standards' checker accepts JSON too. See open question 2.

=== trope-checker's file today

`verification/pwg.json` at `0dceab6` (sha256 `3bf20a2e…5bda`) is canonical JCS.
It is an evidence ledger, though, not a receipt in this form. The prototype
refuses it ("unknown field commit"), and the test pins that refusal. To be read
as a receipt, the file would need these changes. They are work for
trope-checker, not for Mathesis:

* *Envelope.* `repo`, `commit` and the `receipts` map would become
  `repository`, `source_commit`, and receipts nested in their legs. `note`,
  `policy`, `title`, `notes`, `reference` and `declarations` stay in the
  ledger, not in the receipt.
* *Receipts.* `evidence` (the run URL) would become `run`. `checker` and
  `checker_version` would become one `checker` string, as the deed writes it
  (`"agda 2.7.0.1"`).
* *Inputs (hard gap).* `inputs_digest` is a git tree SHA-1 of a directory
  (`git-tree verification/proofs/agda = 8d3370e…`). §8.5.1 wants a SHA-256 of
  the sorted file manifest, *and* the manifest itself. Every `current` receipt
  lacks both.
* *Axioms.* The axioms are bare names (`propext`, `Quot.sound`,
  `Classical.choice`). §8.5.1 needs `{name, disposition}`, and the disposition
  is the owner's call under TRUSTED-BASE-REDUCTION-POLICY, not an agent's. The
  file itself notes that the Lean legs' Mathlib dependency is one PROOF-POLICY
  §2 forbids for a recheck leg.
* *Vocabulary.* Tokens are uppercase (`PROVEN`). `PROVEN-WITH-AXIOMS` is not a
  §5 token in the reader's vocabulary. `NOT-INVENTORIED` is not one of the
  reader's states. Unavailable legs carry a token and a state, where the reader
  wants neither but wants a `reason`. 19 of the 25 Agda legs use route
  `re-proved`, where §8.5.1 wants `source`.
* *Scope.* Six claims have no Agda leg (route `unavailable`), and one claim is
  `engineering`. The reader requires an Agda leg on every claim, and §8.1
  grades theory only. Those claims would be left out of the receipt.

=== Alternatives considered

* *trope-checker emits a deed.* Ruled out by the owner (2026-11-16).
* *Change the canon reader to read JSON.* This is the cleanest long-term
  answer, and the source is the right place for it (doctrine 14). The reader
  is a standards artefact, though, so the change is a standards proposal: split
  `readProofReceipt` into a parse step and a `derive(record)` step that takes
  either syntax. Until then the oracle approach reaches the same record without
  touching the pin. This is the most elegant arm, and the proposal recommends
  it as the destination (open question 1).
* *Read `pwg.json` as it is, with a mapping in Mathesis.* Rejected. It would
  make Mathesis the interpreter of another repository's ledger, guessing
  dispositions and translating vocabularies. That is assigning, not deriving.
* *Let the JSON carry `pwg` and check it, as the deed does.* Rejected, as the
  ruling requires. A JSON grade would be a second thing to keep in step.
  Leaving it out makes "derived, not asserted" structural.

=== Consequences

* A repository that can emit canonical JSON but not a deed has a route into
  the registry, and still cannot assert its own grade.
* Mathesis carries a second syntax for one record. The equivalence tests keep
  both syntaxes tied to the same reader.
* The 900-octet value limit comes from the deed's line limit. A longer
  invocation needs the standards change of open question 1.

=== Open questions for the owner

. Should the destination be a standards change, `derive(record)` in the canon
  reader, with this prototype retired once that change lands?
. §8.5.3 git checks for JSON receipts: should standards' `check-proof-receipt.sh`
  accept JSON? Until it does, a JSON receipt cannot be catalogued at `PWG-1`.
. `result`: keep it, the one field with no deed counterpart, or drop it and
  let `state` carry it, as the deed does?
. The final LF: allow it, as the estate JSON gate does, or require none, as
  bare JCS output has?
