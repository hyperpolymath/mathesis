== The programme in one page

*The question.* The owner's research note on types, tropes and identity asks
one question. When may "a proposition or transition cross between those
spaces without conflating distinct identities or inventing authority,
evidence or preserved particularity"? This programme takes one kind of
crossing first: a mathematical claim that crosses between systems. Mathesis
is the anchor for that kind.

*The crossings.* A claim crosses in five ways:

. from one prover to another (a recheck leg);
. from a theorem to the code that implements it;
. from a theorem to the estate rule that relies on it;
. from a proof to the ledger row that reports it;
. from many ledger rows to one published view.

Each crossing can lose something. The programme asks, for each one, what is
preserved, what is forgotten, and what evidence shows it.

*What the estate has already.* The type family has one algebraic kernel
(`resource-algebra`), three core theories (echo, epistemic and residual
evidence) and several applications. The proof policy has a closed status
vocabulary, a grade ladder and a receipt format. These two bodies of work are
not yet joined.

*The proposal.* Join them, in four lines of work and one horizon:

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Subject |First result to aim for

|1
|The algebra of status
|The ledger tokens are a sub-algebra of the family's bottleneck grade.

|2
|Bridges between systems
|A record of what each existing statement comparison preserves and forgets.

|3
|From theorem to running code
|One vertical slice, with its composite status computed and not typed.

|4
|Evidence for decisions
|A sound three-valued verdict for one synthetic cash forecast.

|Horizon
|Propose and dispose
|A labelled corpus of bridges that an exact checker has accepted or refused.
|===

*The one idea.* Status is a monotone function of evidence. More evidence does
not lower a status. Less evidence does not raise one. A chain of steps is as
strong as its weakest step. Each line of work is this idea in one setting.

== What exists today

This section reports the survey of <<survey>>. It quotes each repository's
own words.

=== One kernel

`resource-algebra` says that it is "a kernel module of the family, not a
family of its own". Its record `ResourceAlgebra` has two operations.
Alternatives (`⊕`) form an idempotent commutative monoid. Sequence (`⊗`)
forms a monoid. The order comes from the alternatives: `a ≼ b` when
`a ⊕ b ≡ b`. The kernel proves that `⊕` is the join of that order. It proves
that each dioid is ordered, so sequence is monotone.

Four instances exist: `Loss`, `MaxPlus`, `HWM` and `MinMax`. The kernel also
states what is *not* true. No strict morphism leaves `HWM` for an algebra
with `𝟘 ≢ 𝟙`. "An occupancy bound is not a cost bound."

`choreographic-types` and `occupancy-types` import the kernel.
`autoformal-fidelity` imports its prelude.

=== Applications of the family

* `autoformal-fidelity` asks: "does a formal artefact F say what its informal
  source S says?" It defines `Faithful = RoundTrip × Covered`. Its statement
  F-2 says: "Round trip is blind to additions".
* `invariant-path` "traces what a claim _means_ when it crosses from one
  domain into another". It says that it is "a claim-path debugger, not a truth
  engine".
* `trope-checker` gives "a sufficiency verdict" for a graded Trope IR. Its
  calculus names "the bottleneck (max–min) grading of a path by its weakest
  link".
* `secret-types` proves noninterference "over any Lattice record". It lists
  the laws that the proof uses, and it shows by countermodels that each law
  is necessary. `lintel` instantiates the theorem.
* `MetaManifold-Evidence` answers "is the taxon present, absent, or
  undecided?" from read counts and a noise bound. Its theorem
  `never-strengthens` says that "more noise never strengthens a verdict".
* `EpistemicTypes.jl` attaches a receipt to each row of a taxonomic table.
* `hvn-dehallucinator` keeps agent task state outside the model. "Only an
  independent verifier may write COMPLETE."

=== Systems, proof tools and languages

* *Systems with theory inside.* `januskey` grades its file operations "in
  resource-algebra's Loss instance". `absolute-zero` proves that a program
  computes nothing, or reveals nothing. `squeakwell` states a recovery ladder
  and says: "No proof lives in this repository." Its proofs are in
  `echo-types`, `verisimdb` and `tropical-types`.
* *Languages.* The Echo fibre is mechanised in six language repositories:
  `my-lang`, `ephapax`, `eclexia`, `tangle`, `phronesis` and `jtv-lang`. Each
  one mirrors or imports the Agda source. `jtv-lang` calls its
  correspondence "`argued`, not bridged". Two of them disagree: `my-lang`
  says that Echo is "NOT a graded comonad", and `eclexia` keeps "the
  graded-comonad-of-loss reading".
* *Quantities.* `affinescript`, `my-lang` and `typell` use the usage
  quantities 0, 1 and ω. `ephapax` says that `resource-algebra` "cannot
  host" them. `affinescript` records an owner ruling: the laws of that
  semiring go in `resource-algebra` "as a non-idempotent tier".
* *Statistics.* Four Julia packages keep Agda proofs of exact identities
  about models: the centred log-ratio, the Benjamini-Hochberg step-up,
  rounding and Fisher's exact test. They state their limits. On control of
  the false discovery rate, `CompositionalDA.jl` says: "Nothing in `proofs/`
  bears on it".
* *Knots.* `tangle` is a language in which "program equivalence is defined by
  isotopy". `quandledb` separates candidate matches from certified
  equivalence. `KnotTheory.jl` has no proof file.
* *Accounts of evidence.* `gnpl` "turns an evidence base into an _account_".
  Its theory document credits provenance semirings and AGM revision.
* *Proof tools.* The survey reader for ECHIDNA, echidnabot, Hypatia and
  proof-burrower stopped at a usage limit. For these four, the programme
  uses only the two ECHIDNA plans in the standards repository. It also uses
  the first line of the proof-burrower README: "Find the mathematical home
  of a proof goal."

=== A method that is already in use

The surveyed repositories share habits that few projects have:

* *Pre-registration.* `choreographic-types` and `autoformal-fidelity` state
  each theorem before the proof exists.
* *Law census.* `secret-types` and `choreographic-types` record exactly which
  laws a proof uses.
* *Necessity by countermodel.* `secret-types` shows that the theorem fails
  when one law is removed.
* *Negative controls.* `residual-evidence-types` keeps 17 rejection controls.
  `firmboot` keeps seven false-claim controls. THY-1 in Mathesis keeps four.
* *Agreement by exhaustion.* `residual-evidence-types` proves by `refl` that
  its checker matches a finite explorer on 546 configurations.
* *Recorded retraction.* `echo-types` keeps a retraction log, and it closes
  one line of work "negatively". `protocol-squisher` is frozen "as its own
  provenance and negative result".
* *Three outcomes.* `proven-tests-and-benches` says: "Three outcomes, not
  two". Its third outcome is "NO CHECK WAS PERFORMED". The verdicts of
  `residual-evidence-types` and `MetaManifold-Evidence` have the same third
  value, and so does the "missing" status of the applied-systems area.

Nobody has written this method down in one place. <<line-0>> proposes that
Mathesis does.

== Line 0: the theorem dossier (method)

*Question.* What must a report of a machine-checked theorem contain? A
stranger must be able to trust it as far as the evidence goes, and no
further.

*Proposal.* Describe the method of the previous section as one checklist, the
*theorem dossier*:

. the statement, registered before the proof;
. the checker, its version, its flags and the import cone;
. the laws and axioms that the proof uses;
. a countermodel for each law that the statement needs;
. the negative controls that the gate rejects;
. the receipts, with their states;
. each bridge, with what it preserves and forgets;
. the retractions.

The registry can then show which parts of the dossier each claim has. This is
a census of practice. It is not a new grade.

*First experiment.* Score ten existing theorems against the checklist: THY-1,
five from `januskey`, two from `secret-types` and two from `lintel`. +
*Pass condition:* each item is found, or its absence is stated, without a
question to the author.

*Who gains.* The owner gains one description of a method that is now spread
over many repositories. Other projects gain a concrete answer to the question
"what does `verified` mean here?".

== Line 1: the algebra of status

*Question.* The estate has five status tokens, a weakest-step rule, a fold
over attempts and a grade ladder. Are these one algebra, and is it one that
the type family already has?

=== What exists

* The tokens: `open`, `argued`, `tested-only`, `proven-with-postulates` and
  `proven` (README and EXPLAINME standard, proof ledger).
* Ledger rule 3: "A row's status may only be as strong as the weakest step it
  depends on."
* Ladder row 2 of the ECHIDNA proof-route plan: "Token order and weakest-step
  meet are lawful". Row 12: the status fold over attempts "is a join on the
  token lattice". Row 7: "Retraction never raises a level."
* Ruling D-10: the theory of rows 2 and 12 lives in Mathesis.
* The `MinMax` instance of `resource-algebra`: alternatives take the minimum
  ("the better path"), and sequence takes the maximum ("the worst hop").

=== Pre-registered statements

The statements use this order, weakest first: `open`, `argued`,
`tested-only`, `proven-with-postulates`, `proven`. PROOF-POLICY §5 lists the
tokens in the reverse of this order.

The owner ruled on 2026-11-10 that the theory starts with this chain and
imports the kernel. The ruling is provisional on one point: the order follows
the result of experiment X-PF1. <<x-pf1>> says what the experiment can
change.

S-1 (the tokens are a bottleneck grade)::
Take the join of the order as alternatives and the meet as sequence, with
`open` as `𝟘` and `proven` as `𝟙`. Then the tokens are a `ResourceAlgebra`
that is a dioid and in which `𝟘` annihilates. The map below is an injective
strict morphism (`Hom`) into `MinMax`.
+
[cols="2,1",options="header"]
|===
|Token |`MinMax` grade
|`proven` |`fin 0`
|`proven-with-postulates` |`fin 1`
|`tested-only` |`fin 2`
|`argued` |`fin 3`
|`open` |`inf`
|===
+
*Consequences.* Rows 2 and 12 follow from the kernel's own lemmas. The join
laws give a fold that does not depend on order or on repetition. The dioid
law gives a monotone weakest step. +
*Falsifier.* `meet(proven, argued) = proven` is derivable (row 2), or two
orders of one fold give two statuses (row 12).

S-2 (positive evaluation is monotone)::
Take an expression built from atoms with `⊕` and `⊗` only. Its value in an
ordered resource algebra is monotone in the grades of the atoms.
+
*Consequences.* A new attempt does not lower a status. A broken leg does not
raise one. Trust withdrawn from a checker version does not raise one. These
are §8.4 rule 4 of PROOF-POLICY and the status half of row 7. +
*Falsifier.* An expression and two atom gradings, one below the other, whose
values are in the opposite order.

S-3 (the grade is a function of the receipt)::
Write PROOF-POLICY §8.2 as a total function from the facts of a receipt to a
grade. The function is monotone in the set of counted legs. The canon reader
agrees with it on each combination of route, token, state and receipt state.
+
*Method.* The domain is finite. Generate the table from the reader, and let
Agda compare it with the function by `refl`. This is the method that
`residual-evidence-types` uses for its 546 configurations. +
*Status aimed for.* The function and its monotonicity are theory. The
agreement of the reader is `tested-only`, by exhaustion. +
*Falsifier.* One row where the reader and the function differ.

=== What experiment X-PF1 can change

*What the experiment asks.* The ECHIDNA proof-route plan states it: "Is a
total order on tokens enough for the weakest-step rule?" The plan models the
meet in scratch Lean. It is not a committed proof.

*Why a chain can fail.* Take a claim with two steps. A written argument
supports the first step. A bounded test supports the second step. The chain
ranks `argued` below `tested-only`, so the claim is `argued`. The label is
correct as a rank: the weakest step has the rank `argued`. The label is wrong
as a description: no argument covers the second step, so the claim as a
whole is not argued.

The same loss exists for alternatives. One argued attempt and one tested
attempt fold to `tested-only`, and the report forgets the argument.

*A candidate that keeps the difference.* Add two values between `open` and
`proven-with-postulates`. The value `mixed` is the meet of `argued` and
`tested-only`: each step has support, and the steps differ in kind. The value
`argued and tested` is their join: the whole claim has an argument and a
test.

[source,text]
----
                 proven
                    |
          proven-with-postulates
                    |
            argued and tested
              /            \
         argued          tested-only
              \            /
                  mixed
                    |
                  open
----

These seven values are the free distributive lattice on the two kinds of
weak evidence, with `open` below and the two proof tokens above. They are a
`ResourceAlgebra` in the same way as the chain: the join is the alternatives
and the meet is the sequence.

*The link to the chain.* The map below sends the seven values to the five
tokens. It keeps `𝟘`, `𝟙`, the join and the meet, so it is a strict
morphism. Each result in the chain is the image of a result in the larger
algebra.

[cols="1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Value |Token
|`proven` |`proven`
|`proven-with-postulates` |`proven-with-postulates`
|`argued and tested` |`tested-only`
|`tested-only` |`tested-only`
|`argued` |`argued`
|`mixed` |`argued`
|`open` |`open`
|===

The chain forgets exactly two differences: `mixed` against `argued`, and
`argued and tested` against `tested-only`. In the terms of `echo-types`,
these are the two fibres of the map that have more than one member.

*How the experiment decides.* List each rule, gate and report in the estate
that reads a token. For each one, ask whether its result changes between the
two members of one fibre. +
*Pass condition:* the list covers the proof-route ABI, the registry and
ledger rule 3. Each entry has an answer and a source.

*Three outcomes.*

[cols="2,3,4,3",options="header"]
|===
|Outcome |Finding |Change in Mathesis |Change in other places

|A. The chain is enough.
|Each use of a token is a threshold on rank. No use tells the members of a
fibre apart.
|None. S-1 stands. The theory records the experiment as the reason for the
chain.
|None.

|B. The chain is enough for gates. It is not enough for reports.
|Gates read the rank only. A person who reads a report needs the kind.
|The carrier of the theory becomes the seven values. S-1 gains one statement:
the map above is a strict morphism onto the chain. The registry shows the
token and the kind, for example `argued (mixed)`.
|The five tokens stay the closed vocabulary of PROOF-POLICY §5. The kind is a
field on the record. It is not a new token.

|C. The chain is wrong.
|One rule accepts tested evidence and refuses argued evidence. A second rule
does the reverse. No chain serves both rules.
|As in B. Each statement that uses the order is also checked against the
partial order.
|The order in the canon is the owner's ruling in the standards repository.
The Idris2 `Token` module of the proof-route ABI changes with it.
|===

A fourth result is possible: the order of two tokens is the wrong way
round. Then two rows of the table in S-1 change places, and the proofs keep
their shape.

*What stays in each case.*

* S-2 does not change. It holds for each ordered resource algebra, so it
  holds for the seven values with no new proof. This is the reason to prove
  it once, against the kernel.
* The embedding of the five tokens in `MinMax` stays true. In outcomes B and
  C it describes the image of the larger algebra.
* The Rocq and Lean legs of a changed statement are proved again. The old
  receipts get the state `superseded`, as PROOF-POLICY §8.4 rule 5 requires.
  No grade is carried forward.
* The table of S-3 is generated again with the new values.

*One more level exists.* The seven values keep the kind of the weak evidence.
They do not keep which step was weak, or which axiom a proof uses. The trust
expression of Q-1.2 keeps those. *Reading:* the three structures are a
tower. The chain is an image of the seven values, and the seven values are
an image of the trust expression. X-PF1 chooses the lowest level that the
rules of the estate need.

=== Research questions

Q-1.1 Is a chain enough?::
This is experiment X-PF1. <<x-pf1>> gives the candidate, the test and the
outcomes. *Reading:* the chain is a lossy view of a richer value, and the
fibre of `echo-types` measures the loss.

Q-1.2 What does a recheck leg buy?::
*Reading:* a theorem with three legs rests on a disjunction. The claim holds
if the Agda kernel is sound. It also holds if the Rocq kernel is sound and
the Rocq bridge is faithful. The same is true for Lean. More legs weaken the
assumption about kernels. Each leg adds an assumption about a bridge. In the
kernel's
terms, legs are alternatives and the parts of one leg are a sequence. The
question is whether this *trust expression* can be computed from a receipt,
and whether the grade is its monotone image. `epistemic-types` already has
the vocabulary: its result L-3 derives a claim from a proof "only from an
accepted sound checker".

Q-1.3 Which conversions between grade schemes are sound?::
PROOF-POLICY §8 lists seven schemes named "grade" or "level" and says: "None
of these converts into another." `resource-algebra` proves statements of this
kind with morphisms and with separations. The question is which maps between
the estate's schemes are morphisms, which are lax, and which a countermodel
refutes. The first map is the one in row 2: token to HYP-01 level.

== Line 2: bridges between systems

*Question.* A statement in one system is said to be the same as a statement
in another. What exactly is preserved? What is forgotten? How can a third
party check it?

=== What exists

* The correspondence states of PROOF-POLICY §2.2, and the rule that "a
  successful compilation in each prover does not establish that all proved
  the same proposition".
* Written statement comparisons in at least five repositories: Mathesis
  (THY-1), `januskey` (T-1), `lintel`, `MetaManifold-Evidence` and
  `hvn-dehallucinator`.
* The theory of `autoformal-fidelity`: a round trip rules out collapses and
  is blind to additions. Faithfulness needs coverage too.
* The classification of `invariant-path`: `ValidPath`, `Overextended`,
  `Conflation`, `Incomplete` and `Abstain`.
* A blocked transport route. THY-1 records that the Agda to Dedukti
  translator fails on one construct, and that the Lambdapi exporters drop
  rewrite rules.
* Two structures named `ResourceAlgebra`, one in Agda and one in Lean, with
  different laws and an open bridge (`resource-algebra`, item M-2).
* A recorded mismatch. `absolute-zero` says that its OND-5 in Agda and its
  OND-5 in Coq and Lean are "different propositions".
* A map inside one home. `verisimdb` says that each of its theorems "is an
  instance of a more general Echo-Types theorem". It says that its bridge
  from Coq to Agda is "Currently NOT IMPLEMENTED".
* A possible repeat. `januskey`, `valence-shell` and `absolute-zero` each
  prove that undo in reverse order restores a state. *Reading* of the survey:
  no file compares the three statements.
* A tested bridge between two implementations of one statistical model
  (added 2026-11-12). `hyperpolymath/MetaManifold-WebUI` at `5a89914` fits
  the two-group multinomial logit with its own Julia code and with the R
  package MGLM. Its `test/unit/test_differential.jl` checks both against one
  closed form, statement S-MN under Q-2.6, and against each other: estimates
  agree to 1e-6 and standard errors to a relative 1e-6. The bridge is
  `tested-only`. No prover is an endpoint, so it is not a record in
  `docs/atlas/claims/bridges.jsonl`, which takes Agda, Rocq and Lean only.

=== Research questions

Q-2.1 What must a statement comparison record?::
The five existing comparisons are prose. Each lists differences of encoding:
universes, the empty type, equality, implicit arguments. Collect the
differences into a closed list. For each one, state when it preserves the
statement and when it adds a side condition.

Q-2.2 Is a round trip enough?::
PROOF-POLICY §2 allows a comparison "by a round trip back through Dedukti".
`autoformal-fidelity` F-2 says that a round trip cannot see an addition.
*Reading:* a bridge that rests on a round trip alone is weaker than a bridge
that also shows coverage. The question is whether the estate's comparisons
show coverage.

Q-2.3 Can a bridge hold by construction?::
For statements in a small fragment, write the statement once and generate the
Agda, Rocq and Lean forms. The bridge then rests on the generator. The canon
has a precedent: `ZenodoDeposits.jl` regenerates its proved table, and the
row is `tested-only`.

Q-2.4 Where does transport fail?::
Keep each failure of the Agda to Dedukti to Rocq or Lean route as a minimal
reproducer with tool versions. The collection is a map of the frontier, and
each entry is a report for the tool's authors.

Q-2.5 When are two legs independent?::
The research note asks when "multiple views count as independent
corroboration, rather than duplicated presentations of one witness".
*Reading:* a transported leg checks the same argument with a second kernel. A
re-proved leg can be a second argument. In a trust expression, two legs are
independent with respect to an assumption when they do not share it. A
comparison that one reader wrote for both legs is a shared assumption.

Q-2.6 Can a statistical oracle become theory?::
Two statements came out of the work on upstream issue #22 of
`MetaManifold-WebUI`. Each holds for any implementation, so each is theory by
PROOF-POLICY §1. Neither is proved; their status is `tested-only`.
+
*S-MN.* Fit the baseline-category multinomial logit to counts in two groups
A and B, with an intercept and a group term, and reference taxon r. Let
S~gj~ be the pooled count of taxon j in group g, and let all four of S~Aj~,
S~Ar~, S~Bj~ and S~Br~ be positive. Then the maximum-likelihood
group coefficient of taxon j is log(S~Bj~/S~Br~) − log(S~Aj~/S~Ar~), and the
inverse observed information gives it the variance
1/S~Aj~ + 1/S~Ar~ + 1/S~Bj~ + 1/S~Br~. Two implementations meet it to 1e-8
(`5a89914`, the test named above).
+
*S-DM.* In Dirichlet-multinomial regression with a log link on every taxon's
Dirichlet parameter, the contrast b~j~ − b~r~ of two group coefficients does
not change when the same constant is added to every taxon's coefficient. So
the contrast does not depend on the direction in which a group's total
concentration grows. As the concentrations of both groups grow without
bound, the contrast tends to the multinomial estimate of S-MN. In one
simulation on multinomial counts, MGLM's contrasts matched the multinomial
ones to about 1e-5 and its standard errors were 0.15% larger. This is an
observation, not a proof. With one group flat and the other overdispersed
the limit is not the multinomial: in a second simulation the contrasts
differed from S-MN by up to 0.13 and the standard errors were 6 to 7 times
larger. So the statement needs both groups.
+
*Reading:* S-MN is an oracle that any implementation of the model can be
tested against. If it were proved, a test that uses it would rest on a
theorem instead of on agreement between programs. S-DM is an equivalence at a
boundary. Its dependant is the rule in both MetaManifold-WebUI branches that
marks Dirichlet-multinomial rows `boundary`, and that calls their estimates the
multinomial ones only when both groups are flat.
Neither statement meets the admission rule of `docs/theory/README.adoc` until
it is proved in Agda and re-proved in Rocq and Lean.

[[line-4]]
== Line 4: evidence for decisions

*Question.* Can the evidence discipline of the proof ledger serve a decision
about money, capacity and people?

=== What exists

* The applied-systems area: five evidence statuses, from "verified
  observation" to "missing", and the rule that a missing input stays blank.
* Its liquidity method: "Missing opening cash makes the cash feasibility
  verdict unknown."
* `MetaManifold-Evidence`: a verdict of `entailed`, `refuted` or `unresolved`
  from counts and a noise bound, with `verdict-sound` and `never-strengthens`.
* `EpistemicTypes.jl`: a receipt for each row, and a merge test that can
  refuse.

=== Pre-registered statements

S-4 (a sound cash verdict)::
Give each weekly receipt and payment as an interval. Give a cash floor for
each week. The verdict is `meets the floor`, `breaches the floor` or
`undecided`. The verdict is sound: `meets` holds for each value in the
intervals, and `breaches` holds for each value in the intervals. A missing
input is an unbounded interval.

S-5 (more uncertainty does not strengthen)::
If each interval grows, a verdict of `undecided` stays `undecided`, and no
verdict changes from `breaches` to `meets` or from `meets` to `breaches`.

*Reading:* S-4 and S-5 have the shape of `verdict-sound` and
`never-strengthens`. The fibre is the set of cash paths that the inputs
allow. If the reading is correct, the proofs reuse `residual-evidence-types`
at a pin, as `MetaManifold-Evidence` does.

=== Research questions

Q-4.1 Is evidence status a grade?::
The applied statuses and the proof tokens are both ordered vocabularies with
a weakest-input rule. The question is whether the applied statuses form a
resource algebra, and whether a map to the proof tokens is a morphism. The
research note warns against "implicit exchange" between measures, so the
default answer is that no map exists until one is proved.

Q-4.2 When may two views be added?::
The accounting method keeps five views and says that they are "not amounts to
add together indiscriminately". `EpistemicTypes.jl` has a merge test with
three results. The question is what the merge test is for cost views.

=== Research questions

Q-H.1 What is the task?::
Given two artefacts, a proposer writes a bridge record: the relation, the
criterion, and what is preserved and forgotten. An exact checker or a
reviewer accepts or refuses it. The labels come from the records of line 2.
The refusals are as useful as the acceptances: each negative control and
each countermodel is a hard case.

Q-H.2 How is a proposer measured?::
The Kautz-ladder plan gives the falsifier for K3: "Every proposal is accepted
(the exact reasoner is not refusing anything) or every proposal is rejected
(the front-end is noise)." A proposer passes only between these two ends.

Q-H.3 What does a recheck leg cost?::
A model proposes a Rocq or Lean proof for a generated statement (Q-2.3), and
the kernel checks it. The count of accepted legs for each hour of work is a
number that the estate does not have today. The number decides whether
`PWG-2ii` across the estate is a matter of months or of years.

== How to tell if the programme fails

A programme that cannot fail is not research. Each sign below would show that
a line is not worth its cost.

* *Line 1.* The proofs of rows 2 and 12 through the kernel are longer than
  direct proofs, or no program is ever tested against the specification.
* *Line 2.* The five existing comparisons do not fit one record without
  invented fields. Or the closed list of encoding differences keeps growing
  after twenty cases.
* *Line 3.* No home repository can state the rungs of one slice.
* *Line 4.* S-4 needs assumptions that no real forecast meets.
* *The whole.* After two quarters, no result of the programme has changed a
  rule, a gate or a document in any other repository.

[[limits]]
== What the programme does not claim

* It does not claim one calculus for the type family. The research note says
  that none is required, and the kernel's own separations show where the
  instances differ.
* It does not claim that a status token, a grade and an evidence status are
  the same thing. Each map between them is a question until it is proved.
* It does not claim that a bridge gives identity. A bridge holds under its
  criterion.
* It does not claim that a recheck leg is an independent witness of truth.
  Q-1.2 and Q-2.5 ask what a leg does witness.
* It does not move any theorem out of its home repository.
* It does not treat a proposal of a model as evidence.
