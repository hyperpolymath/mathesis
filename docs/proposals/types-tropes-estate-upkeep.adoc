= Types and tropes against estate upkeep: findings so far
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 2
:sectnums:
:icons: font

Status: *findings, not ratified* (2026-11-25). This document reports a
research run that is unfinished. It claims no proved result and builds
nothing. Every idea in it carries one of three marks, defined in
<<marks>>. No idea is marked as a working cure, because none has been built.

The question was the owner's: can types and tropes, from Mathesis, from the
estate's own type repositories, from the classical literature, or new ones,
permanently remove the recurring frustrations of upkeep on the GitHub
estate? The scope here is upkeep: CI gates, failing and missing checks,
drift from templates and canon, merges, dependency bumps, and agent errors
that land in those places.

== Summary

* The upkeep record is dominated by *copy fan-out*, not by individual
  mistakes. In one count, 10,095 of 19,443 fix-worded commits carry a
  subject repeated in 8 or more repositories. A fault written once into
  a template or a sweep is then repaired hundreds of times.
* The worst failures are *absences that read as success*. A workflow file
  that does not parse runs none of its jobs: GitHub records a failed run
  with no job and no job log, and a check named after one of its jobs is
  never reported at all. Unless that check is required, nothing blocks the
  merge. In one count, 521 repair commits in 281 repositories fixed workflows of this
  kind, two of them 44 and 117 days after the break.
* Six of the forty ideas were checked by independent verifiers before the
  run stopped. Each pilot measurement was reproduced, and each idea was
  refuted *as written*, though not for one reason. In five, the mechanism
  already exists as a working tool or a canon design, inside or outside the
  estate, so the move is "adopt", not "build". In all six the stated
  invariant fell short: the failure moved to an unproved adapter, an
  unchecked boundary or a person, or a cited law did not cover the real
  obligation. Most claimed "prevents" where the
  honest effect is "detects earlier" or "shrinks". The reasons for each are
  in <<tested>>.
* The estate's maths still matters, in a different role from the one the
  generators gave it. It supplies the *specification* that an adopted tool
  must meet and the *argument* for why a gate is sound. It is not the
  implementation. See <<principles>>.
* The three recommendations are in <<recommendations>>.

[[marks]]
== What the marks mean

[horizontal]
*tested*:: A script or an existing test suite was run against the cloned
estate, and a second agent reproduced the result independently. What was
tested is the premise and the pilot measurement. The cure itself was not
built in any case.
*argued*:: The mechanism rests on a construct that its home repository
records as proved, or on established literature. One agent ran a pilot
measurement over the clones, and no second agent reproduced it.
*open*:: The central claim is a new conjecture, stated but not proved. Pilot
numbers may exist; they bear on the pain, not on the conjecture.

A fact about another repository is that repository's own claim (for
example "PROVED per its MANIFEST"). Mathesis did not recheck any proof for
this document. Prover status follows `3-practice/PROOF-POLICY.adoc` in
standards, and no Proof Witness Grade is asserted here.

== How the run was done, and where it stopped

* *Data.* 484 of the 530 repositories in hyperpolymath, metadatastician,
  catachresis-colloquium and stuff-of-awe were cloned with history
  (93,345 commits, 8,466 workflow files), six private theory repositories
  among them added by hand at depth 50. The other 46 were not read.
* *Understand.* 23 agents read eight theory clusters and mined commit
  subjects, workflows, bumps, merges, agent incidents and forge issues.
  Another four surveyed the external literature.
* *Generate.* Six generators produced 83 candidates, one per ring (estate
  maths, near the estate, classical, recent research, novel) plus one
  generator that started from the costliest pains. A merge step reduced
  these to 40.
* *Verify.* Each candidate was to be attacked through two lenses:
  grounding and soundness, then prior art and real effect. The model usage
  limit stopped the run after 10 of the 80 verdicts: ideas 1 to 4 under both
  lenses, ideas 5 and 6 under the grounding lens only. The portfolio synthesis and the
  completeness critic did not run. This document is the hand-written
  substitute for both.
* *Not durable.* The raw findings (about 1 MB of notes and probe scripts)
  live in the session container, not in any repository.

[[tested]]
== The six verified ideas

Each was refuted as written. Each has a corrected form the verifiers
proposed, and that corrected form is what is marked here.

=== Lock: one writer, one judge — *tested*

* *Idea as written.* The proved Idris2 judge
  (`proven-cicd-and-workflows/src/Proven/Lock`) becomes the only authority over `actions.lock`, and it
  prevents fabricated and dangling pins.
* *What was reproduced.* Judge run on 329 locks: 168 consistent, 160
  inconsistent, 1 not run. The template script fails 209 of them. The two
  deciders disagree on 58 repos.
* *Why refuted.* The judge names none of the 16 unresolved standards pins
  live on 2026-11-24: `RefOk` is a representation rule, not resolution.
  GitHub's own `gh actions-lock --verify` (generally available 2026-10-28)
  does the same checks, adds the reachability check the judge lacks, and
  Dependabot now relocks in the same PR. It has one gap that matters
  here: it does not lock reusable workflows called at `jobs.<id>.uses`
  from another repository (gh-actions-lock#179). The estate's canon
  pins are exactly such calls, and mathesis already edits those lock entries
  by hand for this reason (CHANGELOG.adoc).
* *Corrected form (ring 3, adopt, with one estate check kept).*
  `gh actions-lock` writes and `--verify` checks every step-level action.
  Relock the roughly 160 inconsistent locks once. Until #179 is fixed
  upstream, cross-repository reusable-workflow calls need one estate
  check, which must stay required: reduce the 38 variants of
  `check-lock-sync.sh` to a single canon copy that checks only those
  calls, and that each one resolves. Retire the step-level parts of the six
  hand-writers listed in
  `proven-cicd-and-workflows/docs/proven/LOCK-PIN-CATALOGUE-2026-11-13.adoc`.
  Keep the proved judge as a differential cross-check. Handle unresolved
  standards pins with the ADR-003 ancestor check.
* *Changes.* rsr-template-repo (`scripts/check-lock-sync.sh`, lock
  workflows), proven-cicd-and-workflows (judge demoted to cross-check),
  standards (`ADR-003`, pin scripts).

=== No pass on absence — *tested*

* *Idea as written.* One verdict kernel
  (`proven-cicd-and-workflows/src/Assurance/Verdict.idr`), in which "did not run" is a constructor, so
  absent, skipped and stale gates cannot pass.
* *What was reproduced.* `tests/ci-gate.sh`: 646 passed, 0 failed.
  `tests/pass-on-absence.sh`: 173 passed, 10 failed, all in one section that
  needed a command missing from the container. 251 of 258 required
  identities have a matching job.
* *Why refuted.* The failure moves to two unproved adapters: the profile
  that lists the expected checks, and the admission step. A check left out
  of the profile still passes by omission. And the canon already holds the
  successor design, `docs/estate-reconciliation/A3-estate-gate.adoc` in
  standards (draft, law G3 "no pass on absence").
* *Corrected form.* Finish and ratify A3. Derive the required set from
  intent (A3's check table plus declared and detected capabilities), never
  from observed green runs. Add a totality rule: every workflow file and
  job at the head maps to exactly one observation or to a typed, dated
  exemption. Then a workflow that does not parse is `NeverStarted` and
  blocks. Prove the general membership lemma that ADR-001 lists as open.
* *Changes.* standards (A3, reusable gate), proven-cicd-and-workflows
  (`Assurance`), rsr-template-repo (per-repo profile).

=== Workflows as values — *tested*

* *Idea as written.* A workflow is a typed value with a two-constructor
  `Job` type and a canonical printer, and no writer edits the text.
* *What was reproduced.* 12,177 of 12,185 jobs fit the type; the 8 that do
  not are live defects. A closed schema of about 40 keys admits 7,964 of
  8,005 workflow files.
* *Why refuted.* A documented historical defect is well typed, and
  pre-repair files pass. The proposed round-trip law contradicts a
  comment-preserving printer. The pieces exist: actionlint, the
  `github-actions-models` crate and `yamlpatch`.
* *Corrected form (ring 3, adopt).* actionlint plus a duplicate-key parse as
  a required check, claiming "detects at merge" for parse-dead workflows
  (about three quarters of those repairs) and for timeouts on reusable-call
  jobs. Two cross-file checks the per-file type cannot express: lock
  coverage, and caller-permission subsumption for reusable calls. Typed
  writers remain aspirational.
* *Changes.* rsr-template-repo (`workflow-linter.yml`, which has no parse
  step and was itself destroyed in 22 repos), standards
  (`scripts/check-workflow-duplicate-keys.sh`), kyq (later, as the writer).

=== Template sync as a lens — *tested* (survived one lens)

* *Idea as written.* The mint is a lens; PutGet and GetPut are gates; the
  complement is an Echo residue; updates are three-way merges on values.
* *What was reproduced.* The current / stale / diverged partition above.
  By blob identity, 491 of 9,662 fan-out fix commits (5.1%) were pure
  template-version propagation.
* *Verdicts.* Grounding refuted the soundness claims (the transferred
  theorems hide the real obligation). Reality accepted a reduced form:
  Copier, cruft, Debian ucf and nf-core sync already do this; the design
  should be adopted, not the Python tools.
* *Corrected form.* Add the missing `upgrade` half to rsr-template-repo:
  base = replay of the mint at the recorded pin, merged three ways with the
  child and the new mint. State and test the real safety rule: a path
  changes only if the child still equals the replayed mint at that repo's
  *recorded* pin. The 12,582 stale files are an upper bound on what can be
  refreshed automatically. A file can equal some older template version
  without equalling the one its repo was minted from. And only 27 repos
  record a usable `:template-commit` pin. Recount against each repo's own
  pin before any refresh. Report the files that fail the check and leave
  them for a person.
* *Changes.* rsr-template-repo (`replay-mint.sh`, `chain-mint.sh`,
  `check-mint-drift.sh`, a new `upgrade` recipe), the 12 repos that record
  `(mint-answers)`, then every future mint.

=== Copy versus moment files — *tested* (grounding lens only)

* *Idea as written.* From formal ontology: a licence text depends
  generically on its pattern and must equal it; a proof file or status
  statement depends specifically on its repo and must never equal the
  template's, checked by blob id.
* *What was reproduced.* Proof stubs: `Properties.agda` in 79 repos, 63
  identical to the template stub. In 45 repos every proof-language file is
  a template copy. `LICENSES/MPL-2.0.txt` has 4 contents across 440 repos.
* *Why refuted.* The blob-id law misses the named cases (it catches 4 of 30
  template PROOF-STATUS files and 5 of 13 template READMEs) and wrongly
  flags correct identical files across siblings.
* *Corrected form.* The moment rule is provenance, not inequality: a
  moment file must be derived from the repo's own receipts, or be listed as
  an open obligation. The check must regenerate the file from those
  receipts, or validate it against them. Being different from the mint
  proves nothing, because changing one byte of a copied PROOF-STATUS makes
  it differ. A comparison with the replayed mint, or blob identity for
  token-free stubs, is only a cheap first pass that finds the unedited
  copies. Report licence
  differences flag-only and never edit them.
* *Changes.* rsr-template-repo (`check-mint-drift.sh`, mint stops copying
  proof stubs and status text), Mathesis census (`scripts/census`).

=== Checks keyed by what they read — *tested* (grounding lens only)

* *Idea as written.* A gate declares its read footprint; a sparse checkout
  enforces it; a verdict receipt is keyed by the footprint's content; a
  check runs only when its key is new.
* *What was reproduced.* In 135 repos, 12,929 of 17,362 commits left a
  given check's inputs unchanged.
* *Why refuted.* A sparse checkout does not confine a job: it can still
  read the network, the API and the rest of the history. The skip is then
  unsound for exactly the gates that matter. `skip-duplicate-actions`
  already does tree-keyed skips.
* *Corrected form.* Only for pure checks: export the footprint with
  `git archive` into a directory with no `.git`, run in a digest-pinned
  container with no network and no secrets, and key the receipt by that.
  Classify every gate first (pure, whole-tree, history, diff, API, effect);
  never skip API or effect gates by receipt. The receipt form already exists
  in PROOF-POLICY §8.5.
* *Changes.* standards (reusable workflows, PROOF-POLICY receipts),
  rsr-template-repo (`docs/practice/ci-cost-reduction.adoc`).

[[principles]]
== What recurs: six principles

The forty ideas use a small number of type and trope ideas. These are the
part most likely to last.

. *Absence is a value.* "Missing", "never started", "not run" are
  constructors, and a pass needs every input to be a known pass. Mathesis
  `Applied/Missing.agda`, the Assurance verdict kernel and standards
  `ReportBack.idr` state the same law. The verifiers showed where it fails:
  in the list of what is expected, which must come from intent.
. *Evidence is a trope.* A receipt, a status or a verdict inheres in one
  bearer (a commit, a set of inputs, a blob) and does not migrate except by
  stated transport. Most overclaim incidents are evidence reported about the
  wrong bearer.
. *Copy versus moment.* Some files are copies of a pattern and must stay
  equal to it; others are statements about this repository and must come
  from its own receipts. The mint conflates the two.
. *Values, not text.* Structured files are parsed, changed and printed;
  line-oriented writers cause the "missing semicolon" class here.
. *One writer, one judge.* Each fact has one tool that writes it and one
  that checks it. 38 copies of a lock checker that disagree are the
  counter-example.
. *A gate decides both ways.* A check with no planted failure it rejects is
  not known to be a check.

Reading (the author's view): in each verified case the estate's maths
supplied the specification and the soundness argument, and an existing tool
supplied the mechanism. That division is the honest use of Mathesis here.

[[recommendations]]
== Recommendations

. *Adopt the first-party Actions lock, keeping one estate check.*
  `gh actions-lock` writes and `--verify` checks step-level actions, after
  one relock of about 160 inconsistent locks. The 38 checker variants
  collapse to one canon check, still required, for cross-repository
  reusable-workflow calls, which the first-party tool does not lock
  (gh-actions-lock#179). The proved judge stays as a cross-check.
  Tested premise; days of work; changes rsr-template-repo, standards and
  proven-cicd-and-workflows.
. *Make absence block.* Ratify A3 with the required set derived from intent
  and a totality rule over every workflow, and add actionlint with a
  duplicate-key parse as a required check. Turns parse-dead and never-run
  gates from silent green into blocked merges. Tested premise; weeks;
  changes standards and rsr-template-repo.
. *Give the template its update half.* An `upgrade` recipe in
  rsr-template-repo that replays the mint and merges three ways, plus a
  refresh of the stale files that equal the replayed mint at their own
  repo's recorded pin (at most 12,582; to be recounted). Tested premise; weeks;
  changes rsr-template-repo and minted repos.

== Decisions for the owner

* Whether to accept GitHub's first-party lock tooling as the authority for
  step-level actions, with one estate check kept for reusable-workflow
  calls and the proved judge demoted to a cross-check.
* Which verdict function is normative where the Assurance kernel and A3 §7
  differ (empty set, all-exempt set, unrun advisory checks).
* Whether to resume the verification run (34 ideas unverified, the critic
  and portfolio not run), and on which model and effort.
* Whether the raw findings and probe scripts should be kept, and where.
  They are not in any repository now.
